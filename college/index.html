FAQ

Questions about the college cycle.

When should you hire a college admissions coach?

Earlier is calmer, not more expensive — the price is the same in April as it is in October. Starting in junior spring means the school list is settled and a personal statement exists before senior year begins. Starting in the fall works too: the plan compresses, and Plus and the 24-hour rush exist for exactly that situation.

What does the college cycle look like, month by month?

Spring of junior year: the school list, the testing plan, and a summer that feeds the essay. Summer: the personal statement, drafted while there is still time to throw one away. August to November: supplements school by school, the early action and early decision rounds, and the University of California application at the end of November. December to January: regular deadlines for most schools, aid forms, and the last read before submit.

What does a coach read across a college cycle?

The school list — reach, target, and likely calibrated to this student rather than to a ranking, read against the transcript, the budget, and where the student would actually be happy. The personal statement — drafts, not outlines, round after round. The supplements, school by school: this is where the fall volume lives and where generic writing gets caught, so it is where most families spend the most credits between September and November. And the whole application before it ships — activities list, honors, additional information, and every field nobody proofreads, submitted as a single review while there is still time to change it.

What does one review credit actually buy?

One scoped review of one piece of real work — an essay draft, a school list, a full application (Common App, UC, Coalition, or a school's own portal), or a check-in when something changes — returned in writing on a published turnaround and signed by your coach. Your coach helps you decide where each month's credits do the most good.

How much does a college admissions coach cost?

At Craneweave, $299 a month on Core for four review credits and a 72-hour turnaround, or $499 a month on Plus for eight credits, a 48-hour turnaround, and priority matching. Seniors can take the $1,999 Season Pass, which runs August 1 through January 15 and carries 24 credits. Credits reset each billing month, a 24-hour rush is $50 on any review, and you cancel monthly — no contracts, no consult call. A private consultant typically charges $10,000 or more per cycle.

Do you write the essays?

No. The student writes; the coach reads, judges, and says what to do next. The platform does the groundwork — pulling the file together, prepping the draft so the review starts fast — and a named coach applies the judgment and signs it. Nothing goes out under a student's name that they didn't write.

Which coach will we get?

One matched to college admissions who has been through this process, named on every review. Coaches apply and are vetted against our review standards; they don't simply list themselves here. You can request a re-match at any time, and you can ask for a second opinion — another vetted coach's read on the same work — for one credit, like any other review. A parent and a student can both message the coaching team, and either of you can ask us to stop at any point.

Do you guarantee admission?

No — and you should walk away from anyone who does. Admissions is not a purchasable outcome. What we promise is the work: every essay, list, and application read by a named coach on a published turnaround, early enough to act on it.
