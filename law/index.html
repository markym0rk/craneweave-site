Questions applicants ask.

Do I apply myself, or does a parent sign up?

You apply yourself — as an adult, with your own account. There is no parent or guardian on a law file: the billing, the drafts, and the coach relationship are all yours. If a parent wants to help with the cost, that's between the two of you — the account stays yours.

What does a law school personal statement review cover?

A personal statement review is one scoped, written read of your actual draft, signed by your coach. The statement is two pages, no prompt. Your coach reads for the thing only you can say — then tells you which paragraph is the essay and which four are throat-clearing. À la carte, one essay review is $99.

What else on the law file gets reviewed?

Beyond the statement, three things get reviewed. Addenda — a GPA or LSAT explanation, a character and fitness disclosure, a why-school essay: your coach says which to write and which to skip. The school list — reach, target, likely — calibrated against your numbers and scholarship odds. And the full file, read in one pass before it ships. Each is one credit.

How does rolling admissions change the law school timeline?

Rolling admissions means applications are read as they arrive — a February deadline is not an invitation to apply in February. September and October are for the statement, letters, and a rebuilt résumé. November through January, each application goes in when it's genuinely done. Guided modules get you ready before a credit is spent.

What does "founding cohort" mean for law school admissions?

A founding cohort is a free waitlist. College admissions and BS/MD are enrolling today; law school follows, on the same free waitlist as MBA (Round 2, January). Joining costs nothing, holds your place, and commits you to nothing — we'll email you the moment it opens, at the pricing on this page.

Who will review my personal statement?

Your personal statement is reviewed by a vetted coach who has run this application — matched to your cycle and named on every review. Coaches apply and are vetted against our review standards; they don't list themselves here. You can re-match at any time, or get a second opinion from another coach for one credit.

Do you coach the LSAT?

No — we don't coach the LSAT. Craneweave coaches the application: the personal statement, the addenda, the school list, and the file as a whole. Test prep is its own craft and we'd rather send you to it than pretend otherwise. Your plan does account for your test timing, including a retake if you're planning one.

What counts as one review here?

One review is one scoped, written evaluation of one real piece of work: a personal statement draft, an addendum, your school list, or the full application before it ships. It comes back on a published turnaround with your coach's name on it. That's the unit everything here is priced in, which is why the pricing can be this simple.

Do you guarantee admission or scholarship money?

No — we don't guarantee admission or scholarship money, and you should walk away from anyone who does. What we promise is the work: every essay, list, and application read by a named coach before it ships, on schedule. Your drafts stay yours — used only to coach you, deleted within 90 days — sooner on request, never sold. Full policy.
